# Psychology Translation Review Guide

Use this guide during terminology research and source comparison. It is a checklist for preserving the source's meaning, not a substitute for the book's context.

## Concepts and terminology

- Check whether a word names a broad concept, a specific theory, a measured variable, a symptom, a diagnosis, or an everyday description. Preserve those distinctions.
- Check whether a term has a recognized Chinese rendering in the relevant subfield. A familiar everyday translation may be misleading in a technical passage.
- When a term has multiple plausible translations, compare authoritative Chinese psychology references and the author's local definition. Record the selected form and why; preserve the English at first mention when the distinction matters.
- Do not retrofit modern terminology onto an older author or source. Keep historically specific labels faithful and add context only when needed to prevent misunderstanding.
- Preserve whether the author is defining a term, citing another scholar's definition, or using it informally.

## Claims, evidence, and uncertainty

- Retain attribution: distinguish the author's claim from a cited study's result, a participant's report, and the translator's explanation.
- Preserve qualifiers such as *may*, *might*, *suggests*, *is associated with*, *in this sample*, and *under these conditions*. Do not strengthen or weaken the evidence.
- Keep correlation, prediction, mediation, moderation, and causation distinct. Do not infer causal direction from association alone.
- Preserve contrasts between theory, hypothesis, operational definition, observation, interpretation, and conclusion.
- Translate limitations and competing explanations with the same force as the source; do not smooth them away for narrative flow.

## Research methods and statistics

- Check study design, group labels, time points, comparison conditions, sample sizes, and population descriptions against the source.
- Preserve statistical symbols, values, units, confidence intervals, effect sizes, and significance language exactly. Do not treat statistical significance as practical importance or proof of a hypothesis.
- Keep *random assignment* distinct from random sampling; keep participants, observations, and studies distinct when the source does.
- Preserve whether a result is self-reported, observed, experimentally manipulated, or inferred from a measure.
- Keep scale names, item wording, scoring direction, and cited instruments consistent. Do not translate a scale name as if it were a general construct.

## Prose and notes

- Match the author's register: academic, clinical, narrative, or popular. Improve Chinese rhythm without making a cautious research chapter sound certain or a conversational passage sound clinical.
- Preserve examples, metaphors, and anecdotes when they carry conceptual work. Translate the point naturally and add a short note only if the original reference would otherwise be opaque.
- Preserve quotation boundaries, citations, footnote/endnote references, table headings, and references. Do not silently repair or complete a citation that appears incomplete in the source.
- In the glossary, include source details for contested or consequential terms. Clearly label an unverified rendering as provisional rather than presenting it as an established standard.

## Figures, tables, and navigation

- Decide the figure treatment once per book with the user. For an English original plus Chinese explanation, keep the image identifiable and place translated title, caption, labels, legends, and needed in-image text next to it. For a Chinese redraw, verify every label, number, axis, arrow, grouping, and note against the source page; identify it as a redraw.
- Rebuild tables only from legible cells. Check row and column order, merged headings, units, decimal points, significance symbols, superscripts, and footnotes against the PDF image. Use HTML when Markdown cannot represent the structure faithfully. If a cell is unreadable, flag it rather than infer it from a total or neighboring cells.
- Keep quotations and source page references attached to the right passage. Preserve distinct bibliography entries across page breaks. Make chapter and section references clickable when the output format supports it, then verify the targets.

## Page-by-page source-comparison pass

For every source page in the chapter, check:

1. Is every paragraph and substantive element represented, including captions, notes, and table content?
2. Are negation, comparison, attribution, population, and time frame unchanged?
3. Are causal and uncertainty words no stronger than the source?
4. Do terms match the glossary and the author's definitions?
5. Are values, citations, and cross-references exact?
6. Does the Chinese read naturally without adding claims or diagnoses?

Record one status per PDF page. Use the PDF viewer page number as the stable identifier and add the printed page number when visible. Mark an unreadable page `待辨认`, a known translation error `待修订`, and a fully compared page `已核对`; do not call a chapter complete while any substantive page is still pending. For each error, record a short identifying English excerpt, the current Chinese text, the proposed correction, and its type. If the same error recurs, revisit only the affected pages or chapters. Keep correct wording intact during error-only review.
